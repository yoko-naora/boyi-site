Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #16: 欲擒故纵 (yù qín gù zòng), "Let him go, to catch him."

The story

Zuo Zhuan says this happened over two thousand seven hundred years ago. A duke had two sons, and their mother loved the younger one. She asked for him to be the heir; the father refused. When the elder took the throne, she asked for a city for her favourite, and named the strongest one. He said: anywhere but that. So she asked for Jing, and Jing was given. His brother moved in and called himself the Great Uncle of Jing. A minister measured the wall and said a city that big is not allowed. The earl said: my mother wants it. The minister said she is never full, stop it early. The earl said: he keeps doing wrong, he will bring himself down. Wait. Then the brother took two border districts, and then more, until his land reached Lin Yan. Twice the ministers said: now, he is too big. Twice he said no need. Wrong things do not hold, he said; the bigger, the faster it falls. The brother repaired walls, sharpened arms and set a date. Their mother would open a gate from inside. The earl heard the date, and said one word: now. Two hundred chariots went out, and Jing turned on its own lord. His brother ran to Yan, and then to Gong. There the story stops. Not caught. Not killed. Gone. The old book writes it in six characters, and never once says brother: it calls the elder the earl of Zheng, because he failed to teach him — and then two more words: it was his intention. So he was not cornered. He had waited. But he never pushed, and he never set it up either. Later he made peace with his mother.
