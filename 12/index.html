Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #12: 顺手牵羊 (shùn shǒu qiān yáng), "Take the sheep in passing."

The story

Tuesday, four in the afternoon. One appointment today: the biggest client of the year. On the way you stop for five minutes at a small shop, and the folder on the counter carries someone else's name. You do not read it. At six the big client says no — they signed last week with someone else. Driving home, the only order you have is the small one.

Two thousand six hundred years ago, an army marched east to take a state called Zheng. In a small town on that road, a merchant came the other way with twelve oxen and rolls of tanned hide. He walked up to the army and gave it everything, saying his ruler had heard they were passing. He spoke as if he had been sent. He had not — and behind his back a rider was already going home. The army could not attack, could not surround, and turned around. On the way back it came to that small town again. The town was never the target. The town was the road.
