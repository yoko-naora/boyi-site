Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #20: 混水摸鱼 (hùn shuǐ mō yú), "Catch fish in muddy water."

The story

The old histories say this happened more than sixteen hundred years ago. One man had just put the whole north under one flag. He was the emperor of the north, Fu Jian, and he brought an army south: more than six hundred thousand foot soldiers, and two hundred and seventy thousand horsemen. The south sent one man, Xie Xuan, with eighty thousand. The two armies met at a river called the Fei. The southern army could not cross; the northern army did not need to. Everyone could count, and the north had far more men. Then a message came across the water, from the southern commander: you came a long way to stand with your back to a river, that is not a quick fight — move back a little, let my men cross, and we will settle it here. The northern generals said no: hold the bank, keep them in the water. The emperor said yes: let them half cross, then hit them with the horse. So the order went out: fall back. An army that size cannot step back together. The front rank moved; the back rank could not see why. Somewhere behind them, a voice started shouting that the Qin army was beaten — and that voice belonged to Zhu Xu, a southern general the north had captured years before. One shout, repeated, and the step back became a run. Now the southern army crossed: eight thousand picked men, straight at them. The emperor took an arrow and rode north alone. Behind him the rout fed itself: men trampling men, men into the water — the river was so full of men and gear that it stopped flowing. Those who got away heard wind and cranes and thought the chase was on them. They walked day and night, slept in the open, and the army dissolved. The empire in the north began to come apart. The south did not muddy anything. The north did it to itself, with one order.
