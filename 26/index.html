Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #26: 指桑骂槐 (zhǐ sāng mà huái), "Point at the mulberry, scold the locust tree."

The story

The old account behind this stratagem is quoted in a later commentary on the Records of the Three Kingdoms. In Luoyang, more than eighteen hundred years ago, a young official in charge of the northern district repairs the four gates of his office compound and has five-coloured clubs hung on both sides of the entrance. The rule is plain: break the ban, and wealth and power will not buy an exception. A few months later, a man caught out after the night curfew turns out to be the uncle of a palace eunuch, a favourite of the emperor. His nephew's favour does not save him. Across the capital, people fall in line. No one dares break the ban. One person is punished. Everyone else receives the warning.
