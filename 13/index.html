Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #13: 打草惊蛇 (dǎ cǎo jīng shé), "Poke the grass and the snake moves."

The story

Three quotes for one bathroom, all within five hundred of each other. Two of them are three pages long. The third is eighteen, and that one looks the most honest. So you ask one question — not the price, one line you do not understand: waterproofing, what is included, exactly? He answers in one sentence. Too fast. You ask the same thing from another side, and now he says: "That depends." You have not paid anything yet.

In 133 BC, the Han court tried to end the raids from the north in one move. A trader from the border town of Mayi went out to the Xiongnu with an offer: I can open the city for you. The chanyu believed him and came south with a hundred thousand horsemen. Along the road, in the hills, three hundred thousand Han soldiers lay down and waited. A hundred li short of the city, he stopped. From the ridge he could see cattle and sheep spread across the fields — animals in every direction, not one herder. He did not go on. He sent men to take a watchtower by the road, and the clerk posted there knew where the army was hiding. He turned north before dawn. Three hundred thousand men waited in the cold all day. Nobody came. One question — whose cattle are these? — undid a year.
