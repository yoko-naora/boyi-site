Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #23: 远交近攻 (yuǎn jiāo jìn gōng), "Befriend the far. Take the near."

The story

The old book Shiji says this happened more than twenty-two hundred years ago. One state in the west wanted to attack a state far to the east, but two other states sat in between. A man who had come from the east went to see the king of Qin. Marching past other states to hit a far one, he said, is not a plan. Send a small army and you cannot hurt your target. Send a big one and it costs you more than it costs them. His example: the last king of Qi went south, beat Chu, and opened a thousand li of ground. He kept none of it. It was too far, and while Qi was tired, everyone else came for Qi. The book calls that lending the thief weapons and feeding the robber from your own store. So the adviser put it in four words: befriend the far, take the near. Be good to the near neighbors first, then the far ones start to worry, and once the far one is settled, the near ones can be taken. The king made him an adviser that day. Wei was taken first, Huai fell, and two years later, Xingqiu. Not the far one. Not yet. The near ones came first, because the near ones can be kept.
