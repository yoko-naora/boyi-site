Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #8: 暗渡陈仓 (àn dù chén cāng), "Cross at Chencang in the dark."

The story

There is a saying in Chinese: build the road in the open, cross in secret. More than 2,200 years ago a long war had just ended and the empire was cut into eighteen kingdoms. The weakest share went to the farthest corner, on the other side of the mountains. To get there, the army walked a wooden road pinned to a cliff. When he was through, he burned it behind him — partly to stop raiders, but mostly to send one message: I am not coming back. For four months nothing came out of those mountains, and the man on the other side stopped thinking about him. In the eighth month an order came. They did not rebuild the road. They took an old road, the Old Road — west first, then north — and came out at Chencang. He fought outside the city and lost; he fell back to a second town and lost there too; he fell back again and again, and the land north of the mountains was Liu Bang's. A burned road only tells you where the last army went. It says nothing about the next one.
