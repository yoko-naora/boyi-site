Every Chinese kid learns the 36 Stratagems. This is #17: 抛砖引玉 (pāo zhuān yǐn yù), "Throw a brick, get back jade."

The story

Zuo Zhuan says this happened over two thousand seven hundred years ago. Chu came to attack the small state of Jiao and camped at its south gate. The Chu commander looked at the little city and said: Jiao is small, and careless, and careless means few plans. So he gave an order that sounds like a mistake. Stop guarding the men who go out to gather wood. Let Jiao take some. They did. That day the men of Jiao came out and took thirty of them — thirty, not three hundred; the book says thirty. The next day Jiao raced out again, chasing the woodcutters into the hills. Chu held the north gate, and put men at the foot of the hill. Jiao broke. Jiao had to come to terms under its own wall. That was the brick: thirty men, really sent, really taken. And that was the jade: Jiao walked out of its own gate. Nothing was faked. Chu did not lie — it left something real on the road, and Jiao counted easy as safe. Thirty men were not free. They were the door.
